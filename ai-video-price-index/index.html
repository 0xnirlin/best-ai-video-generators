What AI video costs per second in 2026

One tool lists a clip at $0.84 and another at 310 credits, and you cannot tell which is cheap. This page explains how the prices are built, what pushes them up, and how to compare one clip with a finished episode.

Figures are from the Ziztu AI video price index as it was updated on 14 October 2026. Checked 2 November 2026. Providers change prices without notice, so confirm before you buy.

How per-second pricing works

Most video models charge for each second of output, at a rate set by the resolution. The cost of a clip is the rate times the seconds.

Take MiniMax H3 Turbo. At 480P it is $0.025 a second at API prices, so a 5 second clip is about $0.13. At 1080P the same model is $0.08 a second, more than three times the 480P rate.

Across standard-quality video models on the index, API prices ran from $0.025 to $0.168 a second in October 2026. That is a 5 second clip from about $0.13 (MiniMax H3 Turbo, 480P) to about $0.84 (Kling v3 Pro, 1080P).

Two billing styles hide behind that. Kling reports a flat rate per second. Seedance is billed by tokens, and the per-second cost is worked out from the token price and the number of video tokens a clip uses.

How per-image and audio pricing works

Images are a flat price per image. The lowest on the index is Meta Muse Image at $0.01 an image at API prices, which is 30 credits on Ziztu.

Audio is priced in whatever unit suits it: seconds for sound effects, minutes for music, characters for speech. ElevenLabs Eleven Multilingual v4 is $0.08 per 1,000 characters at API prices. English averages about 5 characters a word, so 1,000 words of narration is roughly $0.40 before any markup.

What drives the price

  • The model. Kling v3 costs more than MiniMax H3 Turbo because it needs more compute. It is stronger on photoreal motion, motion control and lip sync. H3 Turbo is better value for anime-style clips and image-to-video.
  • Resolution. 768P and 1080P bill higher than 480P on most models.
  • Length. Cost scales with seconds, so a 60 second video made of 12 clips of 5 seconds is 12 clips' worth, not one.
  • Everything that is not video. Voices, music, reference images and planning each have their own price.
  • Retakes. Few shots are right first time. Budget for some.

One clip is not an episode Recommended: Ziztu

A clip price covers the clip. A micro drama or anime episode also needs a script, a shot list, a cast that looks the same in every shot, voices, a score and a cut. Each one is another tool or another hour, and every handoff is a chance for a character to change face.

The index prices one worked example: a 60 second episode with 12 shots of 5 seconds at 768P, 6 reference images for the cast, 8 spoken lines, one score cue and 5 Director chats. On Ziztu that comes to 7,750 credits on one balance, with the same characters in every shot. Retakes cost extra. It is a worked example from the rate card, not a quote.

A quick way to compare

  • Write down the seconds you need, the resolution, and how many shots.
  • Add images, voices and music, not just clips.
  • Add a few retakes.
  • Price the whole job in each tool, then compare totals.
  • Read the provider's own pricing page on the day, for example fal.ai pricing or OpenRouter models.
