Free video transcript tools (2026): Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X and YouTube

You have a link to a video and you want the words as text. This page lists the free ways to do that for five platforms, what you paste, what you get back, and which limits to expect.

Checked 31 October 2026. Limits change, so read the tool's own page before you rely on one.

Most social platforms show you a video and nothing to copy. A transcript tool listens to the sound and writes it down, usually with the time each line is said. That helps when you study a hook, write captions, quote a clip, or read a video with the sound off.

The pick below is Ziztu. Each of its five pages is free, needs no signup, and returns timestamps with .txt, .srt and .vtt downloads. Details per platform follow.

A transcript with timestamps. If the TikTok has a caption track, that is used and it is instant. If not, the sound is turned into text with the Whisper model, for videos up to 3 minutes. Copy it or save .txt, .srt or .vtt. Photo posts and private accounts are not supported.

Other options

These have their own strengths. Facts below are what their pages said on 31 October 2026.

  • GetTheScript: says it turns TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook videos into text. Single videos are free with no account. Credit packs unlock batch mode and file uploads. Downloads include plain text, timestamped text, SRT and VTT.
  • Supadata: a free Instagram transcript page for Reels and video posts, with no login for one-off use. Heavy use is rate-limited, and it also sells an API.
  • Eden: a free Instagram Reel transcript tool with no signup or card. It also transcribes YouTube videos, and you can copy the text or download a .txt file.
  • Kollab: transcripts for public Instagram videos, with clean text, a timestamped version and SRT-style rows. Its page says signing in is optional and lets you save your work.
  • YouTube's own transcript: for YouTube videos that have captions, click Show transcript in the video description, then click any line to jump to that point in the video.

A note on using other people's words

A transcript is the creator's own words. Use it to read, search, take notes, make captions for a video you have the right to, or study how a good video is built. Do not republish it as your own, and credit the source if you reuse an idea.
